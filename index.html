The Impact of Mobile Phones on Working Conditions in Peru

This paper examines whether the expansion of mobile phone infrastructure improved job quality in Peru, beyond its effects on employment levels. Using annual ENAHO household survey data from 2002–2011 and a staggered rollout of district-level mobile phone service, the study estimates dynamic treatment effects with the interaction-weighted event-study estimator of Sun and Abraham (2021). The identification strategy exploits the rapid expansion in mobile coverage following the entry of América Móvil in 2005, which substantially increased mobile service availability across Peruvian districts. Preliminary results suggest limited effects on overall employment and unemployment, but provide evidence of improvements in working conditions, including pension enrollment, income above the poverty threshold, and reductions in some forms of underemployment. The findings suggest that communication technologies may affect labor market quality primarily through reduced information and matching frictions rather than large changes in employment quantity.

Mismatch in the Egyptian labor market

This paper examines labor market mismatch in Egypt using the 2023 Egypt Labor Market Panel Survey (ELMPS), focusing on both structural mismatch between labor demand and supply and aspirational mismatch between workers’ expectations and available jobs. We analyze reservation wages and acceptable occupations among the non-employed, as well as education and skill mismatch among workers. Using descriptive analyses, multinomial logit, logit, and OLS models, we show that many job seekers—particularly women—are reluctant to accept the informal private sector jobs that dominate employment growth, even when wages are relatively high. Among the employed, substantial overeducation and skill mismatch persist across sectors and educational backgrounds. The findings highlight the importance of job quality, gender norms, and the limited availability of formal employment in shaping persistent labor market mismatch in Egypt.
